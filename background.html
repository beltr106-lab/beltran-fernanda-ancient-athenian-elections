Background

Demokratia

Voting was an important part of the development of democracy in Athens. Citizens used different voting systems to pass laws, choose leaders, and decide the outcomes of important issues. This commonly included voting through ballots, a randomizing machine, and by a show of hands. These systems helped give Athenian citizens a role in their government. In fact, the Greek word demokratia means people power. However, not everyone could participate as only demos, or free male citizens, were allowed to vote. These methods helped shape some of the earliest forms of democratic government.

Kleroterion

Athenian representatives were chosen by a randomizing machine called the kleroterion, a machine made up of tubes and balls. This would randomly select each tribe's representatives. Choosing people at random was important for a democracy that wanted to give full power to the people.

Laws and Court Cases

Laws and court cases were decided by the Assembly or ekklesia. The Assembly was made up of men, and each person had a say. Usually, eligible Athenian voters would meet in person and issues were decided amongst them before the Assembly that day. Voting was done by raising hands, and was determined by 9 presidents or proedroi, who ran meetings of the council and Assembly.

Stone Ballots

The Assembly handled verdicts in all criminal and civil trials, and jurors would cast their votes using stones as secret ballots. Jurors were chosen also by using the stone kleroterion. Each juror was given two stones, one regular stone and one with a hole in the middle of it. They then would approach two urns and drop one stone with their real verdict in the first urn, and toss the other in the second urn. This method was used for anonymity, as no one could tell which stone was which.

Timeline

594 BCE

Early reforms in Athens allow regular citizens to vote. Solon lays the foundations for Greek democracy.

508-507 BCE

Cleisthenes introduces the concept of isonomia or equality under the law. Additionally, he creates the process of ostracism.

487 BCE

The vote for the first ever ostracism happens, and Hipparchos is exiled from Athens.

416 BCE

The process of ostracism and ostraka script in politics end.
